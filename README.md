## The actual fork in the road: pay by bandwidth, or pay by IP

Decodo's residential line is bandwidth-only. You buy gigabytes; IP usage is unlimited but traffic is metered. 9Proxy sells residential proxies on two separate models, and picking the wrong one is the single fastest way to feel like either provider is bad value.

**Residential by IPs** — you buy a fixed number of residential IPs with no traffic limit at all. Each IP stays usable for a few hours up to roughly 24 hours of natural residential uptime, and the package itself doesn't expire; it's consumed as you forward IPs. Authentication runs through the 9Proxy desktop app using local port forwarding. This is the model for account-bound work: logged-in sessions, cart flows, anything where an unexpected IP change breaks the task.

**Residential by GB** — you buy traffic, not addresses. Endpoints generate without limit, IPs rotate per request or stay sticky for a configured duration, and authentication is username/password or IP whitelisting straight from the dashboard. No desktop app required.

Both models cover country, state, city, ZIP and ISP targeting, and support HTTP, HTTPS and SOCKS5.

> The practical rule: if bandwidth is unpredictable but you need addresses to stay put, buy IPs. If you need thousands of rotating endpoints and small requests, buy gigabytes.

## Where the pricing difference is real

Compare like for like at the tiers both providers actually publish:

- **Small-scale entry:** 9Proxy's 5 GB plan is $15. Decodo's smallest plan is 3 GB at $11.25 — cheaper per month, but you're paying $3.75/GB for it versus $3.00/GB.

That last point is easy to underestimate. If your scraping runs in bursts — a campaign week, then three quiet weeks — a monthly bandwidth subscription can quietly waste a third of what you paid for. Balance-based pricing doesn't have that problem.

## Where Decodo still wins

Three things, and they're not small.

**Coverage.** Decodo runs 115M+ residential IPs across 195+ locations. 9Proxy publishes 20M+ IPs across 90+ locations with 8,000+ servers and a claimed 99.95% uptime. If your targets are in a thin market, the bigger pool has more to draw from.

**Product range.** 9Proxy is residential only. There's no datacenter, no ISP/static residential, no mobile, and no managed unblocker or scraping API. If you need a mix of proxy types on one bill, Decodo covers it and 9Proxy doesn't. This is the hardest limitation to work around and the main reason someone might keep both.

**Free evaluation.** Decodo gives you a 3-day, 100 MB trial plus a 14-day money-back window on paid plans. 9Proxy has no free trial. There is a 60-second refund policy — if a proxy fails within the first minute of activation you get the credit back automatically — and a "Today List" that lets you reuse proxies accessed in the last 24 hours at no extra charge. Both are genuinely useful, but neither is a substitute for kicking the tyres for three days at zero cost.

## Limits of 9Proxy worth knowing before you buy

- **The IP-based model needs the desktop app.** IPs are forwarded through a local port rather than generated in the browser dashboard. That's fine on a workstation, less convenient on a headless server.
- **Residential IP lifetimes are short.** Expect a few hours to about 24 hours per IP naturally. For long-lived sticky sessions, the GB-based plans with sticky mode are the better fit.
- **Speed is steady rather than class-leading.** One independent review describes performance as consistent on US and Western European targets, with visible peak-hour slowdowns in parts of Southeast Asia — normal for residential networks, but worth checking against your own targets.
- **Replacements are partly manual.** The 60-second policy covers fresh failures automatically. An IP that dies an hour later needs a manual claim.

## Who should switch, and who shouldn't

**Switch if** you're burning more than 50 GB a month on residential traffic and the per-gigabyte bill has started to feel like a tax; if your usage is bursty and monthly bandwidth expiry is costing you money; or if your workflow is account-shaped and you'd rather buy IPs with unmetered traffic than watch a data counter all week.

**Stay with Decodo if** your targets need datacenter, ISP or mobile proxies; if you rely on the Site Unblocker or the scraping API; if 90+ countries doesn't cover where you're working; or if you want a free trial before any spend.

**Run both if** you're doing anything production-critical. Routing most traffic through one provider with a smaller share on the other costs a little more per month than picking a winner. It also means one provider's bad Tuesday isn't your outage.

## Practical notes if you're moving

Start with the smallest tier that covers a real week of work rather than the cheapest one on the list. If you're migrating a scraping pipeline, do the first run against your actual targets — proxy performance is target-specific, and generic benchmarks tell you very little about whether *your* site blocks *your* subnet.

Decide the billing model before you top up, not after. Buying gigabytes when you needed sticky IPs, or buying IPs when you needed rotated endpoints, is the most common and most expensive planning mistake in this category.

Then point your existing tooling at the new credentials with SOCKS5 or HTTP(S). Dashboard-based GB plans drop into Scrapy, Puppeteer or Playwright without a local install; IP-based plans require the app running first.
